# Community reel: research and recommended additions

Research date: 29 October 2026. Corridor: **Blaenau Ffestiniog – Llan Ffestiniog – Trawsfynydd**. Purpose: improve the proposed walking, wheeling, cycling and recreation film as a tool for community discussion. This is editorial research and a concept brief, not a feasibility study or an agreed infrastructure scheme.

## 1. Recommendation

Make the public reel answer **“What could this place do for our communities?”** Show local journeys, places to spend time, and connections between the three settlements, supported by the existing geographic model. Keep the inclusive cast already specified in the [Blender build plan](blender-build-plan.md), and give people identifiable activities and destinations.

The most useful additions are a visible Llan Ffestiniog connection, an inclusive place to rest and meet, short local outings, links to public transport, Welsh cultural identity, nature-based recreation, and an invitation to shape the proposal. Include a brief, understandable acknowledgement of railway options and unresolved access rather than presenting a completed conversion as inevitable.

**Evidence labels used below:** “Verified” means supported by the named source, with its date and scope. “Recommendation” is our proposed film/project addition. “Unresolved” needs confirmation; a render must not turn it into a fact. The recommendations are editorial judgments, not findings that a particular design fits the land or has community approval.

## 2. Findings that should shape the story

### A. Use local priorities as the starting point

**Verified:** Gwynedd's April 2025 Bro Ffestiniog Area Plan records consultation concerns about youth provision, public transport and community wellbeing, alongside appreciation of local culture, nature and community spirit. Its action plan includes Llan Ffestiniog development and Welsh-language activity. These are engagement findings, not a representative vote for this railway proposal. [Bro Ffestiniog Area Plan, printed pp. 5–10](https://www.gwynedd.llyw.cymru/en/Businesses/Documents-Busnes/Regeneration/Our-Area/-Bro-Ffestiniog-Area-Plan-1.pdf)

**Recommendation:** show teenagers meeting friends, older neighbours taking a manageable outing, a family pausing, and people reaching village services. Give Llan Ffestiniog a named scene and a proposed village gateway. The middle settlement should be a destination, not just scenery between the endpoints.

### B. There is a current route for public input

**Verified:** Cyngor Gwynedd's active-travel consultation runs **3 September–26 November 2026** and invites residents, businesses and community groups to suggest walking, wheeling and cycling improvements. The page does not establish approval or inclusion of this corridor. [Council consultation](https://www.gwynedd.llyw.cymru/en-gb/have-your-say/live-consultations-and-surveys/active-travel-consultation)

**Recommendation:** if releasing before that deadline, make the film usable as discussion material for a submission. Use a project contact/feedback destination and optionally the council consultation link; neither substitutes for the other. Recheck the closing date and live URL immediately before export. After the deadline, replace that message with the project's continuing engagement route.

**Historical context:** the council's January 2016 consultation report records suggestions for connections north from Trawsfynydd towards Blaenau Ffestiniog. This shows a history of interest in better links; it is not evidence of current consensus or an approved alignment. [2016 report, printed p. 11](https://www.gwynedd.llyw.cymru/en/Council/Documents---Council/Have-your-say/Activetravelmaps/Active-Travel---GC-Exisiting-Map-Final-Report.pdf)

### C. Railway status needs careful wording

**Verified, historical:** a TfW response dated **12 October 2021** describes the Blaenau Ffestiniog–Trawsfynydd Lake Halt line as mothballed and owned by Network Rail. It says TfW had no plans for it at that time. This does not reconfirm 2026 title, every parcel to Trawsfynydd village, or present railway intentions. [TfW FOI response](https://tfw.wales/sites/default/files/2023-03/FOI%202021-288%20Blaenau%20Ffestiniog%20to%20Trawsfynydd%20Lake%20Halt.pdf)

**Verified:** Companies House records the Bala & Ffestiniog Railway Heritage Trust, company 11668316, as dissolved on **27 January 2026**. Its still-visible website describes restoration ambitions. A website remaining online is not proof of an operating organisation; dissolution is not proof that no other rail interests exist. [Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/11668316), [trust's historical proposal](https://balaffest.org.uk/about-us/)

**Recommendation:** use “a community idea for the disused railway corridor” and retain the concept notice. Show existing railway heritage before the proposed treatment. Present walking/cycling conversion, rail-with-trail where physically feasible, and rail retention/restoration as options to investigate with relevant parties. Do not animate viable parallel railway/trail clearances without a survey, imply track removal is agreed, or describe the corridor as ownerless.

### D. Make the transport connections concrete

**Verified:** TfW's Blaenau Ffestiniog walking page identifies the railway station as a starting point and says the T22 bus stops there. [TfW route page](https://tfw.wales/ways-to-travel/walking-and-wheeling/blaenau-ffestiniog)

**Recommendation:** show a person arriving by train or bus, then joining a proposed town-to-trail connection. Add a simple network map distinguishing the live railway, bus interchange, proposed corridor and potential village links. Check the actual approach, gradients and crossings before showing a continuous step-free journey. Do not imply every bus carries conventional bikes or that station bike hire/toilets are available; facility listings need reconciliation and a current check.

### E. Trawsfynydd has existing recreation to connect with

**Verified:** Walk Wheel Cycle Trust describes NCN 82 between Trawsfynydd and Gellilydan as a mixture of traffic-free and other sections with varied surfaces. The lake operator describes an approximately eight-mile cycling loop and warns of a steep section. These descriptions do not establish a continuous accessible shoreline route. [NCN 82 description](https://www.walkwheelcycletrust.org.uk/find-a-route-on-the-national-cycle-network/trawsfynydd-to-gellilydan/), [lake operator cycling information](https://www.trawslake.com/activities/cycling/)

**Recommendation:** show the proposed railway corridor connecting to the **existing wider recreation network**, using a dashed, explicitly proposed connector until surveyed. Reconcile the published route descriptions and mapped geometry before animating a lake circuit; a loop can depart from the shore. Avoid promising a fully traffic-free or universally accessible circuit.

The lake centre lists a café, toilets and cycle hire, with opening information subject to checking. These are possible destinations to research, not facilities on the railway alignment. [Operator facilities](https://www.trawslake.com/facilities/)

### F. Accessibility needs visible design, not only diverse characters

**Verified:** Welsh Government's published 2021 guidance discusses user needs including adapted cycles, pushchairs, surface quality and gradients. The official guidance collection still links that edition; the separate 2025–26 revision consultation page says responses are under review. A final replacement edition was not verified in this research. [Published guidance](https://www.gov.wales/sites/default/files/publications/2022-01/active-travel-act-guidance.pdf), [guidance collection](https://www.gov.wales/active-travel-act-guidance-and-forms), [revision status](https://www.gov.wales/active-travel-act-guidance-atag)

**Recommendation:** depict a firm, even surface; an unobstructed entrance; seating with backs/arms and adjacent wheelchair space; readable wayfinding; and realistic room for passing. Treat these as design intentions. The current 3 m concept ribbon is not proof of comfortable use by every combination of pedestrians and cycles. Compare shared and separated arrangements where demand and available width justify them, with disabled users involved early. Confirm the applicable guidance edition during design.

### G. Safety and belonging matter to who will use it

**Verified:** TfW's 2026 guide for women and girls identifies clear sightlines, maintained routes, usable entry/exit points and routes overlooked by activity as relevant to perceived and actual safety. Its recommendations include involving women and girls in the process. [TfW guide, especially pp. 5–8](https://tfw.wales/sites/default/files/2026-04/Improving-everyday-journeys-for-women-and-girls-V2_ENG.pdf)

**Recommendation:** show legible gateways, an ordinary mix of users, considered visibility and a welcoming rest place. Include a small group of teenage girls as independent users of the proposed space, rather than only as children accompanying adults. Review the imagery with local women and girls. A busy render, a CCTV prop or lights alone do not establish that a route will feel safe.

### H. Nature recreation is locally grounded

**Verified:** Y Dref Werdd's **previous-project** page describes nature walks, outdoor creative activities and children's local heritage/environment sessions across Bro Ffestiniog. This is evidence of local experience, not a promise that those programmes are currently running or will use this route. [Y Dref Werdd project archive](https://www.drefwerdd.cymru/en/previous-projects/hwb-supporting-community)

**Recommendation:** include a quiet nature-learning moment, a small guided walk, or people sketching/observing the landscape in a rest area. This makes “recreation” more tangible than adding more cyclists. Keep activity spaces outside the movement corridor and choose exact locations with local knowledge and ecological advice.

### I. Keep the corridor's ecology visible

**Verified:** NRW describes Ceunant Cynfal near Llan Ffestiniog as an important oakwood/gorge reserve. Its published approach is a **strenuous** walk with significant climbing. The reserve is a nearby destination, not evidence that the proposed corridor itself has the same habitats or a suitable accessible connection. [NRW reserve information](https://www.naturalresourceswales.gov.uk/days-out/places-to-visit/north-west-wales/ceunant-cynfal/?lang=en)

**Recommendation:** retain distinctive vegetation and portray care for habitats, rather than a cleared strip with decorative trees. A wildlife interpretation point can refer to the local landscape after content review. If Cynfal is mentioned, label a separate walking destination with its difficulty; do not depict the gorge as a level cycling spur.

Bat Conservation Trust explains that artificial lighting can disrupt bat habitat and movement. **Recommendation:** avoid a continuously floodlit rural corridor in the concept. Resolve lighting needs at particular access points with both users and ecological specialists; no simple light colour guarantees suitability. [BCT lighting guidance](https://www.bats.org.uk/about-bats/threats-to-bats/lighting)

### J. Welsh identity can make the film recognisably local

**Verified:** the official Slate Landscape site identifies Ffestiniog's slate town, quarries and railway to Porthmadog as a World Heritage component. Eryri identifies Yr Ysgwrn near Trawsfynydd as Hedd Wyn's home. These are cultural context and nearby destinations, not an approved heritage designation for the entire proposed trail. [Slate Landscape](https://www.llechi.cymru/slateareas/ffestiniog), [Yr Ysgwrn](https://eryri.gov.wales/discover/history-and-heritage/yr-ysgwrn/)

**Recommendation:** use Welsh-led narration or resident voices, bilingual titles/subtitles, local place names and modest slate/railway interpretation. Have a fluent local reviewer check the Welsh. Distinguish the live narrow-gauge Ffestiniog Railway to Porthmadog from this former standard-gauge Bala and Festiniog corridor. Cultural destinations off the alignment need separately verified links.

### K. Existing Gwynedd routes provide useful precedents

**Verified:** Gwynedd describes its Lonydd Glas as free-to-use walking/cycling routes established along former railways. Eryri's Mawddach Trail description highlights a wide, even surface, several joining points and options for shorter outings. [Gwynedd recreational routes](https://www.gwynedd.llyw.cymru/en-gb/leisure-country-parks-paths-and-beaches/parks-paths-and-green-spaces/recreational-routes), [Mawddach Trail](https://eryri.gov.wales/walk/mawddach-trail/)

**Recommendation:** borrow the principles of multiple entrances, clear information, flexible outing lengths and a landscape-led experience. Use these as precedents for discussion, not proof of this corridor's deliverability, ownership arrangements, price or likely visitor numbers.

### L. Keep the present-day Trawsfynydd context current

**Verified:** NRS announced on **8 October 2026** that responsibility for the reactor buildings area had been handed to Costain for the height-reduction project. This is a current decommissioning programme, not a completed transformation or a new public recreation area. [NRS update](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/trawsfynydd-takes-next-step-towards-reactor-height-reduction)

**Recommendation:** keep site boundaries and public recreation distinct. If the buildings appear, use a dated reference and the existing decommissioning label. Do not silently replace them with a completed future skyline.

## 3. Ranked additions to the reel

Priorities below are editorial recommendations. **Essential** items should influence the 90-second cut. **Next** items can appear briefly or be developed in a longer consultation film. Detailed layouts remain illustrative until checked.

| Priority | Addition | What the viewer sees | Why it helps / dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Essential | Three-community map | Blaenau Ffestiniog, **Llan Ffestiniog**, Trawsfynydd, with distinct existing/proposed links | Explains who it connects; verify gateway locations rather than assuming station sites are public entrances |
| Essential | Everyday journeys | Someone arriving from town/public transport, a parent on a local trip, friends meeting | Shows practical local use; destinations and last-street connections need mapping |
| Essential | Inclusive use in action | Older walker, wheelchair/handcycle user, children, women and men from varied backgrounds | Carries the agreed casting brief into useful actions and comfortable interactions |
| Essential | Llan Ffestiniog rest/gathering place | A small off-path seating area, wheelchair space, cycle parking and village direction sign | Gives the middle community a role and shows recreation; exact site requires land/access checks |
| Essential | Short outings | A map inset or sign suggesting places to join/turn back | Makes participation possible without travelling the entire corridor; derive distances and gradients from verified links |
| Essential | Considerate passing | Rider slowing and waiting where necessary; walkers and children keeping space | Explains how the space could work; preserve actual widths and do not stage impossible passing |
| Essential | Nature and heritage | A quiet landscape pause and an interpreted railway/slate detail | Adds local character and a reason to spend time; verify each specific feature |
| Essential | A question and next step | A short URL/QR plus an invitation to comment on access, uses and concerns | Turns the reel into an engagement tool; destination must be real and maintained |
| Next | Family nature/play pocket | Children exploring a small nature-learning area while adults rest nearby | Adds recreation beyond travel; keep clear of through movement and sensitive habitat |
| Next | Community-led activities | A small walk, outdoor learning session or cycle-confidence group | Illustrates possible programming; no invented partnership or testimonial |
| Next | Everyday recreation | A jogger, someone walking a dog on a short lead, and people simply sitting or enjoying the view | Broadens the appeal beyond cyclists; stage compatible uses without blocking the route or disturbing wildlife |
| Next | Local enterprise link | People continuing into a village café/shop, simple cycle parking/repair provision | Keeps benefit in the settlements; operational arrangements and any branding need agreement |
| Next | Weather and maintenance | A brief damp-weather shot, believable drainage/firm surface and cared-for edges | Makes the vision credible year-round; do not promise flood-free or all-weather performance without investigation |

If only **five new scenes/assets** can be afforded: prioritise the three-community map, a Llan gateway/rest place, meaningful inclusive walking/wheeling/cycling interactions, one local nature/heritage pause, and a usable feedback end card. Obtain more value from recurring people and one well-designed rest place than from a catalogue of new buildings.

## 4. Proposed 90-second community edit

This is an **alternative public-facing edit**, not a change already made to `REEL_SCENES`. Preserve the existing film as the migration reference. Reuse the geographic windows, but author separate camera/people/caption configurations for the revised story. It remains 2,700 frames at 30 fps.

| Time | Frames, zero-based inclusive | Place and action | Main message |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–8 s | 0–239 | Close, recognisable moments: older neighbours, young friends and a family | Could this corridor become a place for our communities? |
| 8–18 s | 240–539 | Clear map of all three settlements and the existing railway context | A proposed connection: Blaenau Ffestiniog – Llan Ffestiniog – Trawsfynydd |
| 18–30 s | 540–899 | Blaenau: public transport/town gateway and an everyday trip | Connect places people already use |
| 30–44 s | 900–1319 | Llan Ffestiniog: proposed village link and off-path rest/gathering area | Join locally, meet friends, take a shorter outing |
| 44–58 s | 1320–1739 | Maentwrog: walking, wheeling and cycling at human scale | Space for different people and considerate use |
| 58–70 s | 1740–2099 | Trawsfynydd: village, landscape and a clearly proposed existing-network connection | More ways to reach recreation and spend time outdoors |
| 70–78 s | 2100–2339 | Quiet nature/heritage moment | Care for the landscape and remember its story |
| 78–84 s | 2340–2519 | Simple map/options graphic; avoid dense technical text | Explore access, railway options, nature and long-term care together |
| 84–90 s | 2520–2699 | Community invitation, short URL and QR | Where would you join? What matters most? |

Retain the concept badge throughout and appropriate attribution. Narration carries explanation; captions carry the key point rather than paragraphs. Use Welsh narration with English subtitles as one proposed version, and an English-narrated alternative if useful. Caption spoken words accurately and provide a transcript. Check contrast, reading time and a sound-off preview. A QR code supplements a readable short URL and an offline way to contribute.

Use one or two brief **actual, consented local voices** if available; scripted placeholder lines must not be attributed to real residents or presented as testimonials. Local people can also review an entirely animated version without appearing in it. Do not invent survey support, partnerships, promises of jobs, carbon savings, visitor totals or construction costs.

## 5. Community questions and possible contributors

Use the reel to ask open questions, including disagreement and alternative ideas:

1. Where would you join the route, and where would you want to go?
2. Would you use it for a short walk, wheeling, cycling, meeting people, recreation or an everyday journey?
3. What would make it comfortable for children, older people and disabled people?
4. Where are the difficult access points, crossings, gradients or places where users might conflict?
5. What should be retained or improved for railway heritage, wildlife and neighbouring residents?
6. How should railway retention/restoration and walking/cycling options be investigated?
7. What concerns do you have about privacy, dogs, parking, lighting, flooding or long-term maintenance?
8. Which activities or amenities matter most, and what should the project avoid?

Potential contributors to invite into a future process include Ffestiniog Town Council, Trawsfynydd and Maentwrog community councils, affected residents/landowners, the relevant railway owner/operator interests, Gwynedd active-travel/rights-of-way teams, NRW/Eryri where relevant, local schools/youth groups, disabled users, older residents, women and girls, ethnic-minority residents and local businesses. Include people who do not currently cycle and people who favour other corridor uses.

Community organisations worth exploring include Cwmni Bro Ffestiniog, Y Dref Werdd, Antur Stiniog, Seren and GISDA. Cwmni Bro's own site describes a network of local social enterprises and community media. These names are **potential contacts, not supporters or project partners**. Ask who is active and appropriately placed now. [Cwmni Bro Ffestiniog](https://cwmnibro.cymru/)

TfW's rural engagement guide supports linking local journeys with public transport, while its young-people guide stresses involving young people and using accessible language. Use “walking, wheeling and cycling” in public communication rather than relying on technical terminology. Include an offline workshop/paper-map opportunity alongside digital feedback. [Rural guide](https://tfw.wales/sites/default/files/2025-08/Encouraging-walking-wheeling-and-cycling-in-rural-communities_ENG.pdf), [young-people guide](https://tfw.wales/sites/default/files/2024-12/Engaging-young-people-to-improve-active-travel_ENG.pdf)

The immediate project ask should be **help shape the idea and investigate feasibility**, rather than a claim that construction is ready. A phased story can show research/engagement, access and options assessment, then a possible first deliverable section subject to findings. Do not promise a particular opening year or funding route without evidence.

## 6. What the Blender build should add

Keep the existing route/terrain exporter and inclusive character work. Add the following reusable systems:

- **Gateway and connection records:** settlement, geographic position, existing/proposed/unknown status, source, approach geometry and outstanding access issues. Draw unverified connections as diagrammatic dashed links rather than continuous built paths.
- **Rest-place kit:** seating with varied support, wheelchair space, accessible picnic option, simple cycle parking, interpretation and restrained wayfinding. Use measured clearances; one reusable kit can have different local treatments.
- **Activity areas:** gathering/nature-learning space outside the movement corridor, with configurable footprint and evidence/land-status notes. Keep the trail clear.
- **People interactions:** arriving, meeting, sitting, accompanying children, looking at interpretation, waiting and considerate passing, alongside the existing walk/pedal cycles.
- **Presentation layers:** existing-condition view, proposed view, options diagram and source/status legend. Match cameras where comparing conditions. A present-day view must preserve the railway rather than reuse a trail-flattened mesh as if it were existing ground.
- **Community edit data:** the new nine-beat timeline, bilingual text assets, voiceover/subtitle files and an editable end card. Recheck dated claims at final export.

Keep scene additions tagged as proposed, approximate or evidenced as appropriate. Picnic areas, parking, crossings, play features, toilets and repair/hire services are concepts until a location, land permission, design and operator are established. Do not use the railway's former station houses as public cafés or gateways without evidence.

For the **20-second pilot**, prioritise human-scale walking/wheeling/cycling, considerate passing and a modest rest-place glimpse where an appropriate location can be established. Use a separate concept insert if needed; do not distort the Maentwrog data or force every new facility into the existing shots. The full community narrative belongs in the 90-second edit.

## 7. Evidence gaps and editorial corrections

| Topic | What remains to establish | Consequence for the reel |
|---|---|---|
| Railway/land | Present title, boundaries, railway intentions and permissions across each section | No assertion that dissolution or disuse releases the land for conversion |
| Current ground condition | Updated site evidence, structures, drainage, crossings and the recorded building conflict around km 10.93 | No invented continuous obstacle-free route or removal of an occupied building |
| Village gateways | Specific legally usable approaches, slopes and road interfaces at all three communities | A settlement label or station coordinate is not an accessible entrance |
| Demand and mix of use | Local engagement, expected flows and conflict points | The current 3 m ribbon and a populated render do not settle shared/separated design |
| Nature | Corridor-specific habitat/species evidence and ecological constraints | Nearby reserves do not establish what lives at an exact proposed facility site |
| Facilities | Current toilets, hire, opening hours, ownership and operating commitments | Do not render a facility as existing solely because a listing mentions it |
| Lake links | Agreed geometry, surfaces, gradients and access of a connection/circuit | Existing NCN 82 is not proof of an entirely accessible traffic-free lake loop |
| Funding and care | Delivery body, maintenance/inspection responsibilities and current funding eligibility | Present as next-stage work; no invented cost, grant award or opening date |
| Welsh content | Native/local review of narration, place names and sign wording | Treat any draft translation as draft until reviewed |

The earlier `docs/research.md` consultation lead is now directly verified by the council page above. Its categorical statement that no active railway body is pursuing restoration should not be copied into public captions: the verified company dissolution is narrower. Its 2021-era ownership evidence also needs its date stated. This research note records those qualifications without rewriting the historical research file.
