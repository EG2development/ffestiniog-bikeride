# Research summary — Trawsfynydd ↔ Blaenau Ffestiniog cycling concept

Status: early-stage concept research for an animated proposal video. Not an
approved engineering design, cycle route or construction plan. Sources are
listed in `data/source-register.md`; this document is the narrative summary.

## 1. The railway

The corridor is the **Bala and Festiniog Railway** (Great Western Railway),
specifically its northern section between **Blaenau Ffestiniog and
Trawsfynydd** — roughly 13 km (8 miles) of the line's full Bala–Blaenau
length.

**Verified timeline:**
- Company formed 1873; opened as standard gauge, 1 November 1882.
- Vested in the GWR 1910, then British Railways (Western Region) 1948.
- Closed to passengers 4 January 1960; closed completely 27/28 January 1961.
- Reopened 10 April 1964 from Blaenau north to serve nuclear-flask freight
  traffic for **Trawsfynydd nuclear power station** (operational 1965–1991,
  now under Nuclear Decommissioning Authority / Nuclear Restoration Services
  decommissioning).
- Brief passenger specials ran in 1989.
- **Closed and mothballed — not dismantled — on 17 October 1998**, once the
  power station's decommissioning reduced the need for rail traffic.

**Current status (verified):** the track remains largely in situ and the
trackbed is a protected, mothballed Network Rail asset. This is the single
most important correction to the brief this video responds to: it is not a
derelict, ownerless railbed free for repurposing. Several groups have tried,
and failed, to reopen it as a heritage line:

1. **Trawsfynydd Railway Company** (formed 2016) — held a Network Rail
   licence to clear and survey the line; licence revoked August 2017 after
   breaches; company dissolved December 2018.
2. **Blaenau Ffestiniog & Trawsfynydd Railway Society** (2019) — took over
   the vision, held one AGM, was replaced by —
3. **Bala & Ffestiniog Railway Heritage Trust** — aimed to restore the
   Trawsfynydd–Ffestiniog section as an operating heritage railway.
   **Wound down in 2023 "for lack of support"**; formally dissolved at
   Companies House 27 January 2026.

**As of today (October 2026), no active heritage-rail body is pursuing
restoration of this line.** That does not mean the corridor has been
released for other use — ownership and protection status have not changed,
and nothing has been formally transferred. Any cycling-trail concept must be
framed as competing for, not inheriting, a vacant asset.

## 2. Stations, in order (Blaenau Ffestiniog → Trawsfynydd)

See `data/waypoints.json` for coordinates, grid references, and
distance-along-route. Summary:

| Station | Distance from Blaenau | Elevation (DEM) | Present state |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blaenau Ffestiniog | 0.0 km | 217 m | **Still a live station** — southern terminus of the Conwy Valley Line (Transport for Wales) and the heritage narrow-gauge Ffestiniog Railway to Porthmadog |
| Festiniog (Llan Ffestiniog) | 5.3 km | 191 m | Site fully demolished by 2011; mothballed trackbed runs through it, overgrown |
| Maentwrog Road | 8.7 km | 185 m | Station building and stationmaster's house are private residences; A470 bridge rebuilt 2002 preserving the line beneath |
| Trawsfynydd Lake Halt | 10.2 km | 216 m | Built 1934 for anglers/walkers; platform rediscovered under vegetation by 2017 volunteers; never reopened |
| Trawsfynydd | 13.2 km | 247 m | Station building is a private residence; former goods yard/camp sidings now agricultural |

Additional halts/stations on the wider line (Teigl Halt, Tan-y-Manod, Manod,
Tyddyngwyn, Trawsfynydd Camp) exist in the historical record but have not
yet had coordinates verified — see Limitations.

## 3. Scope boundary

The concept **stops at Trawsfynydd** and does not extend toward Bala. The
continuation south/west of Trawsfynydd is a different and more fragmented
story: part of it is submerged under **Llyn Celyn** (the Tryweryn Valley
flooding of 1965, which also drowned Capel Celyn village), and other
sections cross private forestry land with public-access trails reported as
closed. Extending the concept that far would need separate research.

## 4. Existing cycling infrastructure — no overlap

**National Cycle Route 82** passes through Trawsfynydd, but in the *other*
direction: from the village, around the wooded shoreline of Llyn
Trawsfynydd, past the former power station, to Gellilydan (confirmed via the
current Sustrans/Walk Wheel Cycle Trust route page — 4.6 miles, 76% traffic-
free). It does not run north along the old line toward Blaenau Ffestiniog.
**This concept is a genuinely new link, not a retread of a known route.**

## 5. A live opportunity (verify before citing)

Search snippets indicate Gwynedd Council's **Active Travel Network Map
consultation is open 3 September–26 November 2026** — a real channel this
kind of concept could feed into. This was **not confirmed by directly
fetching the council's consultation page**; treat as a promising lead to

## 6. Verified vs. proposed vs. unknown

**Verified:** line identity, opening/closing dates, mothballing date and
reason, station sequence and five stations' coordinates/condition, the
heritage-trust history and its 2023 wind-down, the non-overlap with NCN 82,
the Llyn Celyn flooding boundary south of Trawsfynydd, trackbed geometry and
a DEM-derived elevation range (170–247 m) via the pipeline in
`data/source-register.md`.

**Proposed/conceptual (this project's own ideas, not existing plans):** any
specific cycle-trail surfacing, signage, rest areas, or crossing treatments
shown in the video. None of this has been proposed by any authority.

**Unknown / needs manual verification:** current (2026) on-the-ground
physical condition past ~2017–2020 survey reports; a bridge/tunnel/cutting
inventory; the exact legal owner of the trackbed today (Network Rail as of
2016–2017, not reconfirmed since); coordinates for the smaller intermediate
halts; whether the Gwynedd consultation page actually references this
corridor.

## 7. Cycling route concept (proposed, not existing)

This section is explicitly **this project's own concept**, not a finding —
nothing below has been proposed by Network Rail, Gwynedd Council or any
landowner.

- **Trail treatment:** a shared-use path on the existing trackbed formation
  where it survives intact, sized for the same gentle rail-era gradients
  (the whole 13.2 km corridor only spans 170–247 m elevation).
- **Access points:** the two live/visible ends — Blaenau Ffestiniog station
  (already a public transport hub) and Trawsfynydd village — plus a possible
  mid-route access at Llan Ffestiniog and at the Maentwrog Road / A470
  crossing, subject to the private-ownership status noted in §2.
- **Crossings:** at minimum the A470 crossing near Maentwrog Road needs a
  formal safe-crossing treatment; no other road crossings have been checked
  yet.
- **Connections:** at the Trawsfynydd end, the trail could potentially link
  to the existing NCN 82 Trawsfynydd–Gellilydan route (§4) even though that
  route currently runs the opposite way around the lake — this would need a
  short new link, not shown as already existing.
- **Heritage treatment:** given the unresolved heritage-rail history (§1),
  any real-world version of this concept should assume it is one of several
  competing uses for the corridor, not a default outcome.

## 8. Limitations

- GIS geometry is OSM-derived and DEM-sampled, not a land survey — see the
  gaps list in `data/source-register.md`.
- No stakeholder, landowner, Network Rail, or Gwynedd Council engagement has
  occurred. This document and the resulting video are **a concept pitch**,
  not a feasibility study.
- No permissions, funding, or planning status should ever be implied in the
  video's framing — the heritage-rail history makes this doubly important,
  since the corridor has an unresolved competing-use history, not merely an
  absence of use.
